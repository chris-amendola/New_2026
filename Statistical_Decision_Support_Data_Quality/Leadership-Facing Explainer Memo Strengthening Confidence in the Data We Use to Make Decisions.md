**Audience:** Executive leadership, clinical leadership, finance  
**Purpose:** Alignment and trust  
**Tone:** Plain language, risk-focused, non-technical

---
**Subject:** Strengthening Confidence in the Data We Use to Make Decisions

### Why this matters

Every day, we make high-stakes decisions based on data—about patient care, access, staffing, compensation, and financial performance. Most data issues are not dramatic failures; they are _quiet shifts_ that look plausible and therefore go unnoticed.

The risk is not that data is occasionally wrong.  
The risk is that we act _confidently_ on incorrect information—and never realize it.

---

### What we are changing

We are introducing a small, focused set of statistical checks that continuously monitor whether our data is behaving as expected.

This does **not** replace human judgment.  
It serves the same role as vital signs or reference ranges in clinical care—it tells us when something deserves a closer look.

---

### What this looks like in practice

- We monitor a limited number of decision-critical datasets
    
- We track whether volumes, timing, and key patterns remain consistent with history
    
- When something changes beyond normal variation, we flag it early
    
- Most days, there is nothing to report
    

When there _is_ an issue, we can slow down, investigate, and avoid acting on misleading information.

---

### What this is (and is not)

**This is:**

- A risk-reduction measure
    
- A way to catch silent data issues early
    
- A shared signal across analytics, operations, and leadership
    

**This is not:**

- A criticism of teams or individuals
    
- A promise of perfect data
    
- A complex technical initiative
    

---

### Why now

As our organization grows, the cost of undetected data issues increases. This approach allows us to scale decision-making confidence without scaling manual oversight.

---

### What leadership will see

A simple summary:

- Green: data behaving as expected
    
- Yellow: change observed, under review
    
- Red: confirmed issue affecting decisions
    

If there are no issues, you will hear nothing.

---

**Bottom line:**  
This helps ensure that when we move fast, we do so on information we can trust.
